# PFE Video Script (180 seconds)

> **How to read this.** Simple, clear English — read it calmly and naturally, at a normal
> talking speed (about 2.3 words per second). The whole script is ~410 words, which fits in
> 180 seconds with small pauses. Each block matches a scene in
> `01_claude-design-prompt.md`. Timings are a guide, not a stopwatch.
>
> The video is judged on three things: **a clear context, a clear problem, and clear
> objectives.** This script is built around exactly those three.

---

### Scene 1 — Title (0:00–0:15)

Hello. My name is Achraf Abdelkebir, a final-year student at ESI.
My end-of-studies project is titled
*Decision Support Information System for Logistics Operations and Cost Analysis*.
It was supervised by Mr. Abbas Mohamed Amir, at ESI,
and by Mrs. Rihane Yasmine, at Ourquilane — the company where I carried out the project,
which I will introduce in a moment.

---

### Scene 2 — Context (0:15–0:40)

Let me start with the context.
Today, logistics companies produce huge amounts of data every single day —
every parcel, every trip, every cost leaves a digital trace.
In Algeria, online shopping is growing very fast, and delivery companies are under pressure
to grow smartly and keep their costs under control.
But here is the surprising part: most companies cannot really use the data they already
have. They react to problems instead of seeing them coming.

---

### Scene 3 — The players (0:40–0:55)

This project brings together two partners.
**Ourquilane** is the Algerian software company that design and built IT Solutions.
**Yalidine Express** is the client — the leader of express delivery in Algeria,
with a large network of hubs, centres, and agencies across the whole country.

---

### Scene 4 — The problem (0:55–1:20)

Now, the problem.
Yalidine generates a lot of data: parcels, transport requests, expenses, salaries, and more.
But this data lives in several separate systems that do not talk to each other.
So when a manager asks a simple question — *How are we performing? What does this really
cost? Are we still profitable?* — there is no easy answer.
Someone has to collect the data by hand, from many places, slowly.
The data exists, but it is scattered, slow to use, and hard to trust.

---

### Scene 5 — The question (1:20–1:30)

So the main question of this project is simple:
how can we turn this scattered data into reliable, useful decisions?

---

### Scene 6 — The solution (1:30–1:45)

My answer is a Business Intelligence system.
It collects the data from all the source systems, cleans it automatically,
stores it in one central place, and turns it into clear dashboards and smart alerts.

---

### Scene 7 — Objectives (1:45–2:20)

To do this, the project sets four clear objectives.
**First**, bring all the scattered data together into one single, reliable source.
**Second**, give a deep reading of each activity.
**Third**, monitor proactively, so the team is warned early when an important indicator
starts to drift.
**And fourth**, give each decision-maker the right view for their role, so choices are
based on evidence, not on intuition.

---

### Scene 8 — What it covers (2:20–2:40)

The system covers two main activities: **dedicated transport** and **parcel delivery**.
For each one, it answers three questions:
how are the operations going, how much does it cost and is it profitable,
and how good is the service.

---

### Scene 9 — The outcome (2:40–2:55)

The result is a real change.
Instead of digging through many systems by hand,
managers now open one screen and see a clear, trustworthy picture of the whole business.
They can finally decide with data, and act before problems grow.

---

### Scene 10 — Closing (2:55–3:00)

In short: scattered logistics data, turned into clear and reliable decisions.
Thank you for watching.

---

> **Word count ≈ 410** (≈ 180 s at a calm pace). If you run long, the easiest cuts are the
> second halves of Scene 2 and Scene 4. If you run short, slow down on the four objectives
> in Scene 7 — that is the most important part.
