\uchapter{Abstract}

In an increasingly competitive logistics landscape, the ability to transform raw operational
data into actionable decision-making insights has become a strategic imperative for logistics
service providers. Yet, many organizations continue to rely on fragmented information systems
that lack integration, real-time visibility, and analytical depth --- leaving decision-makers
without the tools necessary to monitor performance and control costs effectively.

This thesis presents the design and deployment of a Business Intelligence (BI) system
dedicated to the analysis of logistics operations and costs within a multi-site logistics
network. The proposed solution is built around a centralized Data Warehouse (DW) as its
analytical backbone, fed by an automated Extract-Transform-Load (ETL) pipeline that
consolidates data from heterogeneous sources, including internal systems and external APIs.
On top of this infrastructure, interactive dashboards and a proactive alerting mechanism are
implemented to support timely and informed decision-making.

The system addresses three hierarchically prioritized business domains. First, transport
demand analysis, covering the evaluation of expressed demands, associated cost assessment,
and pricing support to measure operational profitability. Second, Parcel Cost Control (CCC),
which focuses on tracking logistics costs per shipment, supporting service pricing, and
detecting profitability deviations. Third, route analysis, which explores the integration of
optimization solvers to compare real versus optimized delivery routes and visualize key
performance indicators through maps and comparative tables.

The result is a pragmatic, scalable, and evolvable BI solution that bridges the gap between
raw logistics data and strategic decision support, providing logistics managers with a
reliable foundation for operational excellence and cost governance.
