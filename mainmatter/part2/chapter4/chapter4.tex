\chapter{Conceptual Design and Architecture}

\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Chapter~3 closed on a clear diagnosis: the information needed to steer the business exists, but it
lies scattered across isolated applications, leaving management to decide on intuition rather than on a
consolidated reading of the activity. This chapter is the pivot from that problem to its solution. It
presents LOGIQ, the Business Intelligence decision-support system proposed to close the gap, and sets
out \emph{what} it must do and \emph{how} it is logically structured to do it. The treatment is
deliberately \emph{conceptual} --- a faithful blueprint at the level of functions and logical
structure; the technology stack, frameworks, and deployment are deferred to Chapter~5, which mirrors
this design as its technical realisation.

The presentation follows a single thread, from the abstract to the concrete: the \emph{functional
specification}, the \emph{functional architecture} it implies, and the \emph{technical specifications}
that frame its realisation; then LOGIQ and its \emph{global architecture}; then the three pillars that
give the system its value --- the \emph{data warehouse} at its core, the \emph{ETL pipeline} that
populates it, and the \emph{application platform} through which it is used; and finally the cross-cutting
concerns of \emph{security and conformity}.

\section{Functional Specification}
\label{sec:ch4-functional}

Before describing how LOGIQ is structured, this section sets out \emph{what} it must do. In keeping
with the spirit of Chapter~3, the specification is expressed not as a formal requirements list but as
a \emph{functional design}: the actors that interact with the system, the capabilities it offers
them, and the use cases through which those capabilities are exercised. Every capability is traced
back to one of the specific objectives derived in Chapter~3 (\S\ref{sec:ch3-objectives}), so that the
design answers the diagnosed needs rather than adding features for their own sake.

\subsection{Quality Expectations}
\label{subsec:ch4-quality}

Beyond what the system does, Chapter~3 attached three quality conditions without which it would not be
adopted (Table~\ref{tab:ch3-needs}). They are restated here as functional-level expectations that
shape the design, rather than as a formal non-functional specification:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Reliability} : the figures presented must be trustworthy and consistent across
          views; a decision-support system is only as valuable as the confidence its users place in
          its numbers.
    \item \textbf{Responsiveness at national scale} : the system must stay fast over data covering
          Yalidine's whole national activity, so that opening a view or reading a long history returns
          in seconds, not minutes.
    \item \textbf{Simplicity for non-technical managers} : the interface must be readable and
          navigable by managers who are not data specialists, favouring clear indicators and visual
          reading over raw tables.
\end{itemize}

These three expectations recur as design drivers throughout the chapter: reliability motivates the
layered, quality-gated warehouse; responsiveness motivates the pre-computed aggregate layer and the
separation of analytical from operational data; and simplicity motivates the dashboard organisation
around two axes and three readable views.

\section{Functional Architecture}
\label{sec:ch4-funcarch}

The functional specification said \emph{what} LOGIQ must do; the functional architecture says \emph{how
those functions are organised} into a coherent whole --- still at a purely logical level, before any
technology is chosen. Read left to right, the system is a single, one-directional \emph{value chain}
that turns raw activity into a decision: data leaves the operational systems, is \textbf{processed},
\textbf{consolidated} into one reliable base, and finally delivered to the decision-maker as
\textbf{presentation} and \textbf{alerts}. Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-funcarch} shows this chain and the
modules that make it up.

% [VISUAL-FETCH] functional-architecture block diagram (designed externally; tech-agnostic value chain)
\fig[\textwidth]{functional-architecture}{Functional architecture of LOGIQ: a one-directional value
chain from the heterogeneous operational data sources, through data processing and reliable
consolidation, to the two decision-ready outputs --- presentation and alerts --- consumed by the
decision-maker.}{ch4-funcarch}

The chain begins outside LOGIQ, in the \textbf{data sources and operational applications} --- the
several independent systems that run the business day to day and in which its data lies scattered.
\textbf{Data Processing} draws from them and brings the data into a consistent form, which
\textbf{Reliable Data Consolidation} then holds as a single, coherent, continuously refreshed base ---
the common ground every other module reads, and the point at which the figures become trustworthy.
From that base the chain forks into the two \emph{decision-ready outputs}: \textbf{Data Presentation},
which the decision-maker reads on demand as the two axes and their three views, and \textbf{Alerts \&
Notifications}, which watches the same indicators and pushes a warning the moment one drifts.
Presentation answers questions when they are asked; alerting answers them before they are asked.

Two further responsibilities do not sit at a single point in the chain but cut across all of it, and
are left out of Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-funcarch} for clarity: \textbf{Access \& Personalisation}, which
filters every interaction to the user's role and remembers their saved filters and notification
preferences, and \textbf{Administration}, which governs the users, the roles, and the processing
pipeline that keeps the base trustworthy. Table~\ref{tab:ch4-funcmodules} ties every module --- those
in the figure and these two cross-cutting concerns --- back to the specification family it realises.

\section{Solution and Global Architecture}
\label{sec:ch4-solution}

The previous sections specified, in purely logical terms, \emph{what} LOGIQ must do, how its functions
are organised, and the technical properties its realisation must guarantee. This section makes the
system concrete. It first introduces \textbf{LOGIQ} as the proposed solution --- what it is and the
building blocks it brings together --- and then sets out the \emph{global architecture} that arranges
those blocks into a single, coherent whole. The presentation stays at the level of the solution as a
system: each building block is named here, but examined in depth in its own section further on.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The Proposed Solution --- LOGIQ}
\label{subsec:ch4-logiq}

The answer to the diagnosis of Chapter~3 is \textbf{LOGIQ}, a Business Intelligence decision-support
platform that consolidates the data scattered across the company's operational systems and turns it
into a reliable, navigable reading of logistics operations and costs. The name carries the intent of
the system twice over: \textbf{LOGIQ = Logistics + IQ} --- \emph{logistics intelligence} --- and it
reads as the French \emph{logique}, the promise that decisions rest on logic and evidence rather than
on intuition, exactly the shortcoming the diagnosis exposed. Its visual identity carries the same idea
--- a rising chart riding a delivery cart (Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-logiq-logo}).

% [VISUAL-IMPLEMENT] LOGIQ brand logo, light/primary and dark/reverse variants
\fig[0.85\textwidth]{logos}{The LOGIQ logo, shown in its primary (light) and reverse (dark) variants ---
a chart riding a delivery cart, joining \emph{logistics} and analytical \emph{intelligence}.}{ch4-logiq-logo}

LOGIQ is delivered as a single, full-stack platform that assembles five building blocks, each
realising one of the functional modules of \S\ref{sec:ch4-funcarch} and each detailed in its own
section below:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item a \textbf{data warehouse} --- the consolidated analytical core that holds the whole of the
          company's activity as one coherent, query-optimised source of truth
          (\S\ref{sec:ch4-dw});
    \item an \textbf{automated ETL pipeline} that feeds and refreshes that core on a schedule, drawing
          from each operational system and requiring no manual intervention (\S\ref{sec:ch4-etl});
    \item \textbf{analytical dashboards} that present the activity as the two business axes and their
          three views, through significant KPI charts and indicators (\S\ref{sec:ch4-app});
    \item \textbf{dynamic alerting} --- threshold rules that decision-makers define and \emph{subscribe}
          to, raising a notification through their preferred channel the moment a sensitive indicator
          drifts beyond its acceptable level (\S\ref{sec:ch4-app});
    \item \textbf{role and account management}, where users are not self-registered but \textbf{kept in
          step with the company's HR system} and granted a role that governs which views they may
          consult (\S\ref{sec:ch4-security}).
\end{itemize}

Two traits set the tone for the architecture that follows. First, LOGIQ is \emph{data-centred}: the
warehouse is the gravitational centre, the automated pipeline keeps it current, and everything the
decision-maker sees --- dashboards and alerts alike --- reads from it. Second, LOGIQ is a
\emph{governed} system rather than an open one: its user base is mirrored from the company's HR system,
so onboarding and off-boarding follow the organisation itself, and every interaction is filtered by the
role each user holds. How these traits translate into layers and planes is the subject of the global
architecture, next (Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-globalarch}).

\section{Data Warehouse Design}
\label{sec:ch4-dw}

The data warehouse is the analytical core of LOGIQ: the single consolidated base from which every
dashboard and alert reads. This section designs it conceptually --- the approach, the sources, the
layers, the table inventory, the schema, the modelling techniques, and the scale --- without naming any
database technology, which is deferred to Chapter~5. The full field-level data dictionary is given in
Appendix~A.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Design Approach}
\label{subsec:ch4-dw-approach}

The warehouse uses \textbf{dimensional modelling}, built with the Kimball
lifecycle~\cite{kimball2013}: the model is organised around the business questions to answer, not
around the source systems. For each business process, four steps are applied in order:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Select the business process} --- parcel delivery, transport request, salary run,
          expense record.
    \item \textbf{Declare the grain} --- the exact meaning of one fact row, fixed before any column.
    \item \textbf{Choose the dimensions} --- the descriptive context (who, what, where, when).
    \item \textbf{Identify the facts} --- the numeric measures recorded at that grain.
\end{enumerate}

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Schema Paradigm}
\label{subsec:ch4-dw-schema}

LOGIQ adopts a \textbf{fact-constellation} (galaxy) schema with \textbf{snowflaked} dimensions:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Constellation} --- the seven facts are not forced into a single star but
          \emph{share} a common set of dimensions. Sharing makes figures reconcile (a cost and a
          revenue reported for the same agency and month refer to the same agency and month), and it
          keeps the \textbf{two financial perimeters} apart: no fact mixes a parcel measure with a
          transport measure (TS10).
    \item \textbf{Snowflake} --- every dimension hierarchy is normalised into one table per level, so
          each attribute lives in exactly one place. The rule is \emph{no redundant data}: a value
          stored once cannot disagree with itself. The only exceptions are a few \emph{controlled
          denormalisations} (\S\ref{subsec:ch4-dw-techniques}), each justified by a nullable or
          SCD2-unstable join path.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-dw-erd} gives the complete model; the full field-level dictionary of every table is

\subsection{Scale and Volumetry}
\label{subsec:ch4-dw-scale}

The warehouse is dimensioned for \textbf{production-scale volumes}. The parcel status-event history
dominates --- a massive feed on the order of \emph{tens of millions of rows} --- while the cost side
(payslips, expenses) is smaller and the transport facts are modest. This scale is precisely why the
design takes the shape it does:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item the \textbf{aggregate layer} lets a dashboard read a small summary instead of scanning the
          full fact tables (TS01, TS02);
    \item the refresh is \textbf{incremental} --- only new and changed rows each run, never a full
          reload --- leaving headroom to grow (TS05);
    \item the \textbf{analytical store is kept separate from the operational one} (TS03, TS16), so the
          analytical load never disturbs the application.
\end{itemize}

These three traits are the bridge to the pipeline that populates the warehouse, designed next.

\section{ETL Pipeline Design}
\label{sec:ch4-etl}

The warehouse of \S\ref{sec:ch4-dw} defines the \emph{destination} --- a layered, dimensional base
ready for analysis --- but nothing reaches it by hand. Bridging the operational systems and that base
is the \textbf{integration process}: an \emph{Extract, Transform, Load} (ETL) pipeline that draws data
from each source, brings it into the warehouse's shape, and keeps it current without manual
intervention. This answers the objective of a \emph{reliable, automated refresh} from Chapter~3 and the
capacity and reliability requirements of \S\ref{sec:ch4-techspec} (TS05, TS07, TS17). As elsewhere in
this chapter, the pipeline is described \emph{conceptually}; the orchestration technology and its
configuration are deferred to Chapter~5.

The pipeline's shape is dictated by the warehouse it feeds. Because the warehouse is built in four
successive layers (staging $\rightarrow$ dimensions $\rightarrow$ facts $\rightarrow$ aggregates), the
pipeline advances the data through those same tiers in order, each stage preparing the ground for the
next. Two ideas organise the design: \emph{how} the work is structured and sequenced --- an
asset-oriented orchestration --- and \emph{which} properties make it dependable at national scale.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Orchestration Philosophy --- an Asset-Oriented Pipeline}

The pipeline is designed not as a linear script of imperative steps (``run this, then that''), but
around the \textbf{data assets} it produces. Each table in the warehouse is modelled as an
\emph{asset} that \emph{declares the assets it derives from}: a fact declares the dimensions it needs,
an aggregate declares the facts it summarises, and so on. From these declarations the orchestrator
reconstructs the \textbf{lineage} of the entire warehouse --- what depends on what, end to end
(Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-etl-lineage}).

This asset-oriented view yields design advantages a plain script could not:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Correct ordering by construction} --- assets are materialised in dependency order
          automatically; the sequence is never hand-wired.
    \item \textbf{Safe partial re-runs} --- any single asset (or sub-graph) can be rebuilt on its own,
          without reprocessing the whole warehouse --- essential for recovery and for refreshing only
          what has changed.
    \item \textbf{Transparency and lineage} --- the dependency graph is itself a readable map of the
          warehouse, making the pipeline auditable and far easier to reason about and debug.
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Extract} --- pull data from each source through its API, handling pagination and

    \item \textbf{Stage} --- land the raw extracted data, untransformed, into the staging layer (one
          staging table per source endpoint). This decouples extraction from modelling: the rest of the
          pipeline reads from staging, not from the live sources.
    \item \textbf{Load dimensions} --- conform and normalise the descriptive data into the dimension
          layer, applying Slowly Changing Dimension logic where an entity's attributes change over time.
    \item \textbf{Load facts} --- resolve each business event against its dimension keys and compute its
          measures, writing the result into the fact layer.
    \item \textbf{Refresh aggregates} --- rebuild the pre-computed summary views of the aggregate layer,
          so the dashboards read decision-ready figures directly.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Engineering Properties}

What makes the pipeline \emph{dependable} --- rather than merely functional --- is a small set of
engineering properties, each answering a concrete demand of operating at national scale
(Table~\ref{tab:ch4-etl-properties}).

% [VISUAL-IMPLEMENT] ETL engineering properties mapped to their rationale
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\rowcolors{2}{white}{tblrowalt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.7cm}|>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}X|}
\hline
\rowcolor{tblheader}
\textcolor{white}{\textbf{Property}} & \textcolor{white}{\textbf{Why it matters}} \\
\hline
Incremental extraction & New and changed rows only, resumed from a high-water mark rather than reloaded
in full --- the only tractable option at tens of millions of events. \\
Trailing re-window (parcel status) & A parcel's status keeps changing after creation, so a pure
high-water mark would miss late updates. Each run therefore \emph{clears the last 7 days} of status
history and reloads that window incrementally, so retroactive status changes are always captured. \\
Idempotent loads & Loads follow an \emph{insert-or-update} strategy, so re-running a stage never
duplicates data; the pipeline is always safe to retry. \\
Resilience & Failed source calls are retried with exponential backoff, and a stalled source is skipped
rather than aborting the whole run, so a briefly unavailable system never breaks the refresh. \\
Scheduling & A full refresh runs automatically on a nightly schedule, with a lighter, more frequent
dimension refresh --- no manual intervention is needed to keep the warehouse current. \\
Observability & Every run emits lifecycle events (start, success, failure, cancellation) to the
platform, which records each run and notifies users when fresh data is available or a refresh fails
(\S\ref{sec:ch4-app}). \\
Source decoupling & Because extraction depends only on each source's API contract --- not its internal
schema --- the same pipeline connects to the real production systems unchanged (TS17). \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}
\caption{The engineering properties that make the ETL pipeline dependable at national scale, each traced
to the requirement it answers.}
\label{tab:ch4-etl-properties}
\end{table}

Together these properties close the integration loop: the pipeline fills the warehouse reliably and
automatically, recovers cleanly from failure, and signals its own state to the decision-makers who
depend on its output. Its \emph{source decoupling} in particular makes the design portable --- validated
against faithful simulations of the source systems (Chapter~6), it would connect to the live systems
without modification. With the warehouse defined and the process that feeds it in place, the chapter
turns to the application through which decision-makers finally consult that data.

\section{Application Platform Design}
\label{sec:ch4-app}

The warehouse holds the consolidated data and the pipeline keeps it fresh, but that value is realised
only when a decision-maker can actually \emph{read} it. The \textbf{application platform} is the layer

\subsection{Proactive Alerting}

Presentation answers questions when they are asked; \textbf{alerting} answers them before they are
asked, turning the platform from descriptive into \emph{proactive}. A decision-maker defines a
\textbf{rule} on a sensitive indicator by choosing a metric, a comparison operator, a threshold, a
severity, and a \emph{cooldown} (the minimum quiet interval between repeat alerts). A background worker
evaluates the active rules on a schedule; when a metric crosses its threshold and the cooldown has
elapsed, an \textbf{alert} is raised and \emph{fanned out} to the channels the user has enabled ---
\emph{in-app} in real time, \emph{email}, or both (Figure~\ref{fig:ch4-alert-flow}).

\subsection{Resilience --- Graceful Degradation}

Finally, the platform is designed to \textbf{fail safe}. Because the analytical and operational planes

rather than showing an error (TS08). The application stays usable --- for genuine operation and for
demonstration alike --- even when its analytical source is momentarily out of reach.

With the data presented and made proactive, one cross-cutting question remains: guaranteeing that each
user sees only what their role permits, and that the platform as a whole is secure and conformant ---
the subject of the final design section.

\section{Security and Conformity}
\label{sec:ch4-security}

Every layer designed so far --- warehouse, pipeline, application --- handles the company's data, and
that data is sensitive on two counts:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Commercially confidential figures} --- costs, margins, and volumes for the whole
          national activity.
    \item \textbf{Personal data} --- of clients (names, addresses, telephone numbers, declared parcel
          values) and employees (payroll).
\end{itemize}

Security and conformity are therefore not a feature bolted on at the end but a \textbf{cross-cutting
concern} that constrains the whole design. This section sets out, at the conceptual level, \emph{who}
may see \emph{what} (access
control) and how the design stays aligned with the security and data-protection rules that govern
Yalidine (conformity). It realises the security requirements specified in \S\ref{sec:ch4-techspec}
(TS09--TS11); the concrete mechanisms (tokens, certificates, libraries) are deferred to Chapter~5.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Access Control}

Access control rests on two principles. First, a \textbf{governed user base}: nobody self-registers.
Accounts are mirrored from the company's HR system and \emph{activated} by an administrator, so the
right to use LOGIQ follows the organisation itself --- joining, moving, and leaving the company are
reflected in the platform without a parallel account-management process (TS09). Second,
\textbf{role-based access control (RBAC)}: each user holds a \emph{role}, and the role --- not the
individual --- determines which sections of the platform they may consult. Because permissions are
attached to roles and defined at view level, the administrator can create new roles or adjust existing
ones as the organisation evolves, without touching the system (\S\ref{sec:ch4-functional}).

\subsection{Conformity and Data Protection}

LOGIQ operates inside an established regulatory context, and its design is shaped to remain conformant
with two anchors in particular:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{ISO/IEC 27001}~\cite{iso27001} --- the information-security management framework
          within which Yalidine operates. The platform is designed to sit inside it: controlled access,
          segregation of duties, traceability, and protection of data in transit are treated as design
          obligations rather than optional features.
    \item \textbf{Algerian Law n° 18-07} (10 June 2018)~\cite{loi1807} --- the national law on the
          protection of natural persons in the processing of personal data. Because LOGIQ processes the
          personal data of clients and employees, its design respects the law's core principles ---
          \emph{purpose limitation}, \emph{data minimisation}, and \emph{security of processing}.
\end{itemize}

the warehouse, the pipeline, and the application, to the cross-cutting guarantees that make it
trustworthy. What remains is to show how this blueprint was \emph{built}, the subject of Chapter~5.

\section*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}

This chapter was the pivot from the problem diagnosed in Chapter~3 to its solution. Starting from the
diagnosis --- information scattered across isolated applications, leaving management to decide on
intuition --- it built, step by step, a conceptual blueprint of \textbf{LOGIQ}, the Business
Intelligence decision-support system proposed to close that gap.

The construction followed a single thread, from the abstract to the concrete. It began with the
\emph{functional specification} --- the actors, capabilities, and use cases traced back to the
objectives of Chapter~3 --- and derived from it a \emph{functional architecture}: a one-directional
value chain turning raw activity into decisions. The \emph{technical specifications} then framed, along
the ISO/IEC~25010 quality characteristics, the non-functional properties the realisation must guarantee.
From there the chapter introduced LOGIQ and its \emph{global architecture}, before designing in depth
the three pillars that give the system its value --- the \emph{data warehouse} at its core, the
\emph{ETL pipeline} that populates it, and the \emph{application platform} through which it is consulted
--- and closed on the cross-cutting guarantees of \emph{security and conformity}.

Two principles run through the whole design. LOGIQ is \textbf{data-centred}: the layered dimensional
warehouse is its gravitational centre, the automated pipeline keeps it current, and every dashboard and
alert reads from it. And it is \textbf{governed}: its user base is mirrored from the company's HR system
and every interaction is filtered by role, so the system serves many decision-makers through one,
trustworthy interface. Throughout, the design was kept deliberately \emph{technology-independent} --- a
faithful blueprint, valid before any particular framework is chosen.

That choice now sets up what follows. With \emph{what} LOGIQ is and \emph{how} it is logically
structured established, Chapter~5 turns to \emph{how it was built}: the technology stack, the realisation
of each design block, and the deployment that brings the platform into operation.
