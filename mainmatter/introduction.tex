\section*{Context}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Context}

The logistics industry stands at a decisive turning point. As global supply chains grow more
intricate and competitive pressures intensify, the ability to transform raw operational data
into timely, actionable insights has shifted from a competitive advantage into an operational
necessity. Each day, millions of data records are generated across supply chains from multiple
systems, and the proliferation of digital technologies, IoT devices, and advanced tracking
systems have compounded the challenge of data management~\cite{kpmg2024}.

Yet despite this data abundance, many organizations remain analytically blind --- unable to
convert their own information into decisions that drive profitability. The numbers paint a
sobering picture. Research shows that approximately 89\% of organizations struggle with data
integration, and 61\% of companies find that their rapidly growing data volumes limit their
ability to fully exploit their datasets~\cite{enveyo2025}. More strikingly, only 7\% of
supply chains currently possess the capability to access live data, analyze it, and enable
rapid decisions~\cite{enveyo2025} --- leaving the vast majority of logistics operators
reacting to problems rather than anticipating them. Meanwhile, companies that have embraced
Business Intelligence and advanced analytics report slashing supply chain costs by up to 10\%
and reducing inventory by as much as 25\%~\cite{intelligentaudit2024}, illustrating the
transformative potential that remains largely untapped.

This challenge is particularly acute in emerging markets. Algeria's logistics sector operates
in a context of rapid digital transformation and surging e-commerce activity. The number of
registered e-commerce businesses in Algeria has grown at an average annual rate of 92\% since
2020, while online payment transactions for goods and services tripled between 2020 and
2024~\cite{unctad2025}. Revenue in Algeria's e-commerce market is projected to reach
USD~1.50~billion in 2025~\cite{statista2025}, placing mounting pressure on logistics providers
to scale their operations intelligently and cost-efficiently. Yet, fragmented distribution
systems and limited use of technology in the distribution and logistics sector result in
inefficiency and unreliability --- a structural bottleneck that hampers the sector's ability
to keep pace with demand.

It is within this dual context --- global data complexity and local market dynamism --- that
the present project takes shape. Most supply chain technology environments remain fragmented,
with ERP, TMS, WMS, and planning systems operating on different data models, update cycles,
and integration patterns. Even when each system performs as intended, the combined environment
often responds slowly because coordination across systems is limited~\cite{logisticsviewpoints2026}.
The consequence is clear: decisions are delayed, duplicated, or suboptimal --- a reality that
erodes both operational efficiency and financial performance.

\section*{Problem Statement}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem Statement}

In response to this landscape, the company Ourquilane --- a digital services provider based
in Algiers --- has undertaken to equip its key partner, Yalidine Express, one of Algeria's
leading logistics operators, with a state-of-the-art Business Intelligence system aligned
with international standards. Operating a vast multi-site logistics network, Yalidine Express
generates massive volumes of heterogeneous data on a daily basis: transport requests, delivery
costs, parcel tracking records, route performance metrics, and pricing data. This information
is currently dispersed across multiple disconnected systems, making consolidated analysis
cumbersome, slow, and heavily reliant on manual effort.

The central challenge lies not in the absence of data, but in its fragmentation and
inaccessibility. Decision-makers are unable to rapidly assess the profitability of operations,
detect financial deviations before they escalate, or evaluate the efficiency of delivery
routes without labor-intensive data extraction processes. In a market where e-commerce volumes
are growing exponentially and logistics margins are under constant pressure, this analytical
gap represents a serious strategic vulnerability. The fundamental question driving this project
is therefore:

\begin{quote}
    \itshape
    How can a unified, automated, and scalable Business Intelligence architecture transform
    fragmented logistics data into a reliable foundation for proactive, cost-aware
    decision-making?
\end{quote}

\section*{Objectives}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Objectives}

To address this challenge, this thesis proposes the design and deployment of a comprehensive
and evolvable Business Intelligence system built around three core technical pillars. First,
a centralized Data Warehouse, consolidating data from Yalidine's internal systems and external
APIs into a single, coherent analytical repository. Second, an automated ETL pipeline ensuring
the reliable, repeatable, and high-quality flow of data from heterogeneous sources into the
warehouse. Third, an interactive dashboard and proactive alerting layer, translating stored
data into visual, real-time decision support tools for logistics managers.

These technical components serve three hierarchically prioritized business domains. The
primary axis --- classified as \textit{Must Have} --- focuses on transport demand analysis:
evaluating expressed demands, assessing associated costs, and supporting pricing decisions to
measure operational profitability. The secondary axis --- \textit{Should Have} --- addresses
Parcel Cost Control (CCC), encompassing granular cost tracking per shipment, pricing support,
and detection of profitability deviations. The tertiary axis --- \textit{Could Have} ---
explores advanced route analysis through the integration of optimization solvers, enabling
comparison between real and optimized delivery tours and their visualization through maps and
comparative KPI tables.

To achieve these objectives, the work follows a rigorous methodology: an in-depth
bibliographic study to master BI and dimensional modeling concepts; an analysis of the
existing data landscape at Yalidine Express; a structured needs analysis using the MoSCoW
prioritization method; architectural design of the Data Warehouse schema and ETL flows;
development and testing of dashboards and alerting modules against real data; and, finally,
full system deployment delivering an operational, production-ready decision support solution.

\section*{Thesis Structure}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Thesis Structure}

This thesis is organized into two main parts, framed by a general introduction and a general
conclusion.

\textbf{Part~I --- State of the Art} establishes the theoretical and technological foundations
of the work. Chapter~1 surveys the field of Business Intelligence, covering dimensional
modeling, Data Warehouse architectures, and ETL design principles. Chapter~2 examines the
logistics domain, presenting key concepts in supply chain management, transport cost analysis,
and the current state of analytical tools in the logistics sector.

\textbf{Part~II --- Contribution} presents the practical realization of the system.
Chapter~3 details the requirements analysis and system design, including the MoSCoW
prioritization, the data source inventory, and the dimensional schema of the Data Warehouse.
Chapter~4 describes the implementation of the ETL pipeline and the data integration
architecture. Chapter~5 presents the interactive dashboards and the proactive alerting
mechanism, illustrating their application to each of the three business domains.

The thesis closes with a general conclusion summarizing the contributions, discussing
limitations, and outlining directions for future work.
